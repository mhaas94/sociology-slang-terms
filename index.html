What is slang?

Slang is informal, often playful language used within a group to add humor, emphasis, or a sense of belonging. It usually doesn't belong in formal writing — but it thrives in everyday conversation and online.

Where it comes from

New slang grows out of real-life interactions and pop culture. Today it spreads fastest on social media platforms like TikTok.

Who creates it

Young people have always driven slang, using new words to set themselves apart from older generations. Subcultures and marginalized communities are major sources too — especially Black Americans through African American Vernacular English (AAVE), and LGBTQ communities through drag culture, media, and activism.

Is slang "bad"?

Older generations sometimes see slang as disrespectful, but it isn't inherently negative. It's all about context — knowing when a word fits and avoiding terms that are rude or hurtful.

Think about it

Use the timeline to explore these questions.

  1. Pick one decade. How do its slang words reflect the major events, music, or technology of that time?
  2. Several decades have multiple words for "cool" or "excellent." Why might every generation need its own word for the same idea?
  3. Many terms came from Black and LGBTQ communities before going mainstream. What happens to a word's meaning — and to credit for it — when it spreads beyond the group that created it?
  4. Find a word that shows up in more than one decade. Why do you think it survived or came back?
  5. Which 2020s words do you predict will still be used in 20 years? Which will sound as dated as "cat's meow"?
